# Adjacent Tactics Boards and Team/Video Coaching Platforms: Features Diamondboard Could Borrow (as of Nov 2026)

Research scope: general and multi-sport tactics boards, football/basketball playbook tools, team-management and video-coaching platforms. Research date: 2026-11-23. Most sources are App Store listings, vendor pages and third-party aggregators. App Store text is written by the developer and can lag the shipped product. Where sources disagree, both are given.

## Tactics boards and playbook apps: standout features and how they implement them

### Takeaway
The best tactics boards share one core loop: build a multi-frame (step) animation, shape movement paths with curves, show trails or trajectories, control playback speed, and **export to MP4 and image/PDF**. Basic drawing is usually free. Export, saved projects and no-watermark output are paywalled. Football and basketball playbook tools add **printed playbooks, wristbands and play cards**, and send plays to players' devices with tracking of who studied them. Diamondboard already has steps, drag, freehand, a library, saved plays, undo and notes. The biggest gaps are curved paths with trails, video/image/PDF export, wristband/card printing and shareable plays.

- Another TacticalPad listing exports to a "Document with images and notes", an image, a video or email — [App Store (Futsal & Handball)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id551766745)
- Pricing: free with in-app purchases. The Year Subscription is $24.99/yr and includes upcoming features and TacticalPedia content. The "Export animated plays as video files" IAP is listed at $0.00. Latest version is 2026.1.2 (Jan 2026) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id512949303). ### Inferences
- The common pattern is free drawing, with export (MP4, hi-res image, PDF) and no-watermark output behind Pro. Diamondboard's steps already map to "frames". Speed control, curved paths and trail display are the standard extras.
- Printed wristband and play-card layouts are a distinctive football feature that suits baseball/softball well (signs, defensive coverages, bunt and pickoff plays). Football Play Card shows this can be done with no server, using the browser print dialog plus CSS print layouts.
- "Template every card" (Hudl) and "load boards from other projects" (TacBoard) suggest letting coaches start a new play from a library play or a saved formation.
- A "Recycle Bin" (TacticalPad) is a cheap safety net for local saved plays that have no cloud backup.
- Narrated "face-and-voice" export (Sply) is a niche differentiator.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Apple Pencil-specific features (pressure, palm rejection, Scribble) for any tactics board from these sources.
- No board was confirmed to export GIF. Every confirmed export was MP4/.mov, image or PDF.
- I did not find documented layers, tags or search in the tactics boards reviewed (TacBoard Coach tier has "session search" only). I did not research QR-code sharing, presentation mode, or XO-style football tools beyond what is above.
- Exact prices for TacBoard, easy2coach and Sply were not found.

## Team and video platforms: coaching-communication features to interoperate with or imitate

### Takeaway
Team platforms (GameChanger, TeamSnap) own the roster, schedule, messaging and lineups. Video platforms (CoachNow, OnForm, Dartfish) own annotated video feedback, voice-over, side-by-side comparison and read receipts. Coach's Eye is discontinued, and Hudl moved Technique users to OnForm. A client-only whiteboard can't match their messaging or receipts. It can imitate roster-based labels, "send this play to the team" through native share, and coach voice-over on exported video.

### Inferences
- Features to imitate without a server:
  - Roster-based labels: name or number on each token, kept as a local roster (TacticalPad has rosters and lineups).
  - "Install" a play to the team via the OS share sheet, carrying a link or an MP4/PDF.
  - A per-player view, such as highlighting one position's assignment across all steps (like Hudl's position-specific study).
  - Coach voice-over on an exported animation (CoachNow, OnForm, Sply).
- Read receipts, study tracking, quizzes with results, and group broadcasts all need a backend. They are out of scope for a client-only PWA unless a share-out workflow is good enough (for example, the coach texts a link in the GameChanger or TeamSnap team chat).
- Interop: these platforms don't publish open play-diagram import formats in the sources found. The realistic integration is to export MP4, PNG or PDF, or a link, and post it in their chats.

### Gaps
- I found no public APIs or import formats for GameChanger, TeamSnap or CoachNow that would accept whiteboard plays.
- I could not verify the scope of TeamSnap's "Assignment Management" feature (likely chores or volunteer duties, not play assignments).
- I did not deeply verify Dartfish's full feature set or current pricing.

## Feasibility in a client-only PWA (no server)

### Takeaway
Most "share and export" features can be built client-side on iOS Safari:
- Web Share with files works on iOS 14+.
- Canvas captureStream plus MediaRecorder can produce MP4 (H.264) on iOS 14.5+.
- Printing to PDF works through window.print and CSS.
- URL-encoded plays are possible with no server.

Main risks are WebKit MediaRecorder bugs and the length limits of URL-encoded plays.

### Cited Findings
- Safari supports MediaRecorder from iOS 14.5 and outputs MP4 (H.264/AAC). Fragmented MP4 came in Safari 18.4. This is a secondary source — [TestMu AI](https://www.testmuai.com/web-technologies/mediarecorder/); [WebKit blog: MediaRecorder API](https://webkit.org/blog/11353/mediarecorder-api/)
- `canvas.captureStream()` is supported on iOS Safari 11 through 26.x per caniuse. Older WebKit bugs report blank video on iOS — [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/wf-capture-stream-canvas); [WebKit bug 181663](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=181663)
- A WebKit bug (Mar 2025) reports MediaRecorder failing with video/mp4 variants on the iOS 18.4 beta. I couldn't confirm whether it was fixed — [WebKit bug 290002](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=290002)
- `navigator.canShare({files})` and `navigator.share` with files are supported on iOS Safari 14+ (through 26.6 and 27.0+). They need HTTPS, and web.dev advises feature-detecting with canShare — [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_navigator_canshare_data_files_parameter); [web.dev](https://web.dev/patterns/files/share-files)
- Browser print-to-PDF is a working pattern for wristbands and play cards in a shipping web product — [Football Play Card KB](https://knowledge.footballplaycard.com/how-do-i-download-plays-to-pdf)

### Inferences
- **Video export:** draw each step's interpolated frames to an offscreen canvas, then record with captureStream plus MediaRecorder. Feature-detect `MediaRecorder.isTypeSupported('video/mp4')`, and fall back to WebM on other browsers or to a PNG sequence/PNG per step. A fallback such as frame-by-frame encoding with WebCodecs or an ffmpeg.wasm build is heavy, and its iOS support was not verified here. Diamondboard renders SVG, so frames need SVG-to-canvas rasterization (serialize to an Image, then drawImage).
- **Image export:** SVG to PNG via canvas, then the share sheet or a download.
- **PDF playbook and wristbands:** a print-only route with `@media print` CSS and `@page` sizes, letting the user choose "Save as PDF" or AirPrint. No library is needed.
- **Share links:** compress play JSON (for example deflate plus base64url) into the URL fragment so nothing hits a server. A QR code can be generated client-side for dugout handoff. Long plays may exceed practical URL and QR sizes; a file-share fallback is a `.json` or custom-extension play file shared through the Web Share API and re-imported with a file input.
- **Import/export of the whole library:** JSON file backup and restore is important because local storage on iOS can be evicted. Inferred from the "Recycle Bin" and cloud sync that competitors offer for safety, not directly sourced.

### Gaps
- I didn't confirm the iOS 18.4 MediaRecorder MP4 regression fix, behavior in iOS 26, or Brave on iOS (which uses WebKit) for MediaRecorder or Web Share. These need on-device testing.
- I didn't research practical URL-length or QR-capacity limits.
- I didn't verify the exact iOS version needed for Safari's Web Share file support from Apple's own release notes (caniuse used instead).

## Pricing and freemium models in this segment

### Takeaway
Freemium is the norm. Drawing and animating are free, and export, saving, no-watermark output and premium content go behind a cheap annual subscription (about $20–25/yr for single-coach boards). Team and playbook platforms charge more ($199/yr for Hudl Play Tools add-on). Video-coaching platforms charge about $10–70/mo. Team-management apps are free to coaches and monetize parents and fans.

### Cited Findings
- TacticalPad: free with IAP. Year Subscription is $24.99; Ice Hockey variant is $19.99/yr — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id512949303); [App Store Ice Hockey](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id696728071)
- TacBoard (TheManagerBoard): Free, Pro and Coach tiers, billed annually. Export and save/load are Pro — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/themanagerboard-tacboard/6763226753)
- easy2coach Tactics: Pro removes the watermark — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/easy2coach-tactics/6760240730)
- Soccer Coach Lineup: TacBoard is free on iPad — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/soccer-coach-lineup-tacboard/6749962256)
- Hudl Play Tools: $199/yr. A third party describes it as an add-on to a Hudl subscription — [Hudl](https://www.hudl.com/products/playtools); [vendor comparison](https://football-playbook-sage.vercel.app/resources/best-football-playbook-software-2025)
- Fastmodel/FastDraw pricing comes from Hudl sales — [Hudl FAQ](https://www.hudl.com/products/fastmodel/faq)
- CoachNow+ about $24.99/mo (annual); OnForm Coach about $29.99/mo and Academy $69.99/mo; myDartfish Express has a 15-day trial plus IAP — sources as cited above
- GameChanger is free for teams and paid for fans (Plus about $9.99/mo; Premium about $99.99/yr). TeamSnap has a free tier and Premium at $10/mo billed annually — [gc.com](https://webflow.gc.com/pricing); [TeamSnap](https://teamsnap.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- If Diamondboard ever monetizes, the segment's norms point to free core boarding plus a modest annual "Pro" for export, wristband printing, or extra library packs. A client-only PWA has no App Store IAP, so this would need web payments plus license keys, which conflicts with the no-backend constraint. For launch, a fully free app is a competitive position against $20–25/yr boards that paywall export.
- Watermarked free exports (easy2coach) are a common soft-upsell pattern.

### Gaps
- Exact current prices for several apps (TacBoard, easy2coach, Sply, Dartfish, FastDraw) were not available. Aggregator prices conflict and may be stale.
- No baseball-specific tactics-board pricing was covered here; that is out of scope and handled by other researchers.
