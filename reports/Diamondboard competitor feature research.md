# Ship sharing before launch, learning after

Diamondboard's most dangerous competitor is **Simple Fielding**, a free, account-free, browser-based baseball and softball defense board. It already ships the three things Diamondboard has only planned: share-by-link, a "pause and ask where you go" learning mode, and softball-specific content. Its native iOS app is listed as "coming soon" ([Simple Fielding](https://simplefielding.com/)). The rest of the field is thin. Few dedicated baseball whiteboards are actively maintained, most App Store entries have only a handful of ratings, and several have been abandoned since 2017–2021. That leaves room for a polished, reliable product. The features competitors treat as table stakes are almost all about getting a play off the iPad: links, PNG/PDF/video export, and pushing plays to players. Every one of these except tracking who viewed a play can be built client-side in iOS Safari with no server. With about ten weeks left before February 1, 2027, the highest-leverage pre-launch work is: share links in the URL fragment, image and print/PDF export through the share sheet, a local backup/restore and "recently deleted" safety net, install guidance for Brave users, and a first version of Position Study built on the existing step model. Video export, the full spaced-repetition Quiz, softball and youth field variants, rosters, and anything that needs a server (coach-visible quiz results, read receipts, sync, licensing) belong on the post-launch roadmap.

## Simple Fielding and a handful of web-first boards define the field

The market splits into four groups. Hand-drawn whiteboards are the first: Baseball Board ($4.99, iPad), Baseball Board – Tactics (freemium), the Pitch MetRx whiteboard, and Baseball Blueprint's Clipboard. Animated defensive playbooks are the second: Simple Fielding, Dugout Edge, X's & O's, InMotion Playbooks, Softball Instincts. Situational-IQ quiz trainers are the third: The Baseball Mind, Baseball IQ Pro, BASIQs, Freeze Baseball. Adjacent multi-sport boards and team/video platforms that set user expectations without covering baseball diagrams are the fourth: TacticalPad, TacBoard, Hudl, GameChanger, CoachNow, OnForm. The table summarizes the competitors that matter most.

| **Baseball Board – Tactics** | Free with ads; $2.99–$6.99 IAP or Pro Lifetime | Per-step notes, curved/dashed lines and zones, PNG/PDF/**video** export, browser link share, AirPlay, 50-step undo/redo, offline, 40 drills, rosters, practice plans ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6763575410)) | High: closest interaction model |

Simple Fielding matters most because it is nearly a mirror image of Diamondboard: free, privacy-first, iPad-focused, and covering both baseball and softball. Its repository shows it is built by a small team with plain HTML/SVG and Playwright tests. The notes could not confirm whether it works offline or installs as a PWA ([GitHub](https://github.com/mrthames/simple-fielding)). The category's weakness is also Diamondboard's opening. BASIQs Softball, rated **3.3★ from 66 ratings**, has not been updated since **November 2021**, and reviewers cite repetitive questions, wrong answers, and sign-up friction around a child's birthday and email ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/basiqs-softball/id1034912896)). Baseball WhiteBoard was last updated in 2017, and its one review complains of "terrible navigation… no tutorial, can't insert your own text" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/baseball-whiteboard/id317282434)). Dugout Edge is still in beta, and The Baseball Mind's team tools are still "on the way" ([Dugout Edge](https://www.dugoutedge.com/chalkboard), [Baseball Mind](https://www.thebaseballmind.com/)).

Diamondboard's defensible combination is clear. It is free with no account and no paywall on saving, which Dugout Edge charges for. It installs as an offline PWA; among the baseball-specific competitors, only Baseball Board – Tactics also claims offline use. It has autosave and undo, which a TacticalPad reviewer explicitly asked for ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tacticalpad-coachs-whiteboard/id512949303)). Its library of **~150 plays organized by position** is larger than Simple Fielding's 86 or Dugout Edge's 50+. And it has per-step coaching notes, which only Baseball Board – Tactics clearly matches. The rest of this report covers how to defend that position before Simple Fielding's native app arrives.

## Getting plays off the iPad is the table-stakes gap, and it needs no server

Coaches use these tools to answer one question: "where do I go after the ball is hit?" They use them in pre-practice walk-throughs, between innings, and as homework. A fastpitch coach on DiscussFastpitch asked for exactly this resource, with the example of where the left fielder goes on a ball down the right-field line with a runner on second ([DiscussFastpitch](https://discussfastpitch.com/threads/good-resource-for-learning-how-to-coach-moving-without-the-ball.46674/)). Dugout Edge and Simple Fielding both name absent assistant coaches and parents as audiences ([Dugout Edge](https://www.dugoutedge.com/chalkboard), [Simple Fielding](https://simplefielding.com/)). Those audiences are not holding the coach's iPad, so a board that can only show its screen leaves half the job undone. That is why sharing is the single feature most worth finishing before launch. Simple Fielding and Baseball Board – Tactics both share plays as links that open without an app. Baseball Board and Baseball Board – Tactics export PNG and PDF. Baseball Board – Tactics and TacticalPad export video ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6763575410), [TacticalPad](https://apps.apple.com/app/id512949303)).

The browser platform now supports all of this client-side. **CompressionStream has been in iOS Safari since 16.4** ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_compressionstream)). A play's JSON (tokens, steps, notes) can be deflated, base64url-encoded, and placed in `location.hash`. The fragment never reaches the server, which suits GitHub Pages hosting and the privacy promise. The link format should carry a version byte for future schema changes and include a small bundled fallback, because early 16.4 builds had compression bugs ([WebKit bug 254021](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=254021)). **Web Share with files works in iOS Safari 15+** ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)). Feature-detect with `canShare({files})` and fall back to a download, as web.dev recommends ([web.dev](https://web.dev/patterns/files/share-files)). PNG export is SVG rasterized onto a canvas and handed to that share sheet. A printable playbook needs no library: Football Play Card already ships wristband and play-card PDFs through the browser's print dialog and print CSS ([Football Play Card](https://knowledge.footballplaycard.com/how-do-i-download-plays-to-pdf)), and FastDraw's "Create Playbook" prints one frame per page from a library selection ([FastModel](https://support.fastmodelsports.com/support/solutions/articles/9000231300-creating-playbooks-on-fastdraw)).

Video export is technically possible but is the riskiest piece. Safari has written MP4 from MediaRecorder since iOS 14.5 ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/11353/mediarecorder-api/)), and caniuse lists canvas `captureStream` through iOS 26 ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/wf-capture-stream-canvas)). But WebKit bugs have reported blank canvas recordings ([bug 181663](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=181663)) and MP4 codec failures in the iOS 18.4 beta ([bug 290002](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=290002)), and no source confirms it works cleanly on iOS 26. iOS also has no `getDisplayMedia`, so screen recording from inside the app is not an option ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)). Video should be a post-launch stretch goal, with a PNG-per-step export as the fallback. Two unknowns need a device test before the share design is final: practical URL lengths in iMessage and QR codes, and whether iOS accepts a `.json` play file through the share sheet. Neither was resolved in the research.

One sharing feature cannot be built client-only. Hudl Play Tools ($199/yr) "installs" plays to specific players and shows who studied which play ([Hudl](https://www.hudl.com/products/playtools)), and CoachNow PRO sells read receipts ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id596598472)). Both need accounts and a backend. Because GameChanger and TeamSnap publish no play-import formats, the realistic interop is to post a Diamondboard link or PNG in the team chat coaches already use. That covers most of the value without a server.

## Learning modes should quiz players on the coach's own plays

Quiz apps sell large generic question banks. Baseball IQ Pro has about **8,200 questions**, gives free users 3 games a day, and sells coach tiers up to $249.99 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6759932185)). The Baseball Mind has **1,100+ situations** and a Smart Practice mode that brings missed questions back ([Baseball Mind](https://www.thebaseballmind.com/)). The research found no product that quizzes players on their team's own animated playbook. Diamondboard's position-organized library and per-step notes make it the natural candidate to fill that gap. A fastpitch coach described BASIQs as "more quiz-based than graphics / diagrams," which suggests coaches treat quizzes and diagrams as complements, not substitutes ([DiscussFastpitch](https://discussfastpitch.com/threads/good-resource-for-learning-how-to-coach-moving-without-the-ball.46674/)).

The interaction is already proven. Simple Fielding's lessons stop at the hit, ask "where do you go?", grade the direction the player drags, and then play out the answer ([Simple Fielding](https://simplefielding.com/)). Freeze Baseball ($1.99) freezes the ball and has the player drag all nine defenders, then shows a "Why" panel ([Freeze Baseball](https://freezebaseball.com/)). Learning research supports the same design. A meta-analysis found practice tests outperform restudying ([WSU summary](https://education.wsu.edu/documents/2018/01/rethinking-use-tests.pdf/)), and spaced retrieval beat massed retrieval with a strong effect (**g = 0.74**) across 29 studies ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9)). Studying worked examples helps most early on ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10950551)). The notes left two things uncertain: which Springer paper reports which effect size, and whether these classroom findings transfer to motor or tactical sport learning.

That evidence points to a two-stage build. **Position Study** is the worked example: play a library play step by step with the chosen position highlighted and its per-step note shown. It reuses the step engine and notes, and it competes directly with Simple Fielding's single-player focus and X's & O's position breakdowns ([xsandos.team](https://xsandos.team/)). It is small enough to ship before launch. The note template should follow the coach's "you need to do this and this is why…" framing ([DiscussFastpitch](https://discussfastpitch.com/threads/coach-defensive-responsibilities.46209/)). Each step should also color-code fielders as ball, base, or backup, the framework USA Baseball's skills matrix uses for ages 10–12 ([BBL guide](https://cdn1.sportngin.com/attachments/document/8348-3379948/BBL_Baseball_Development_Guidelines.pdf)) and the role-coloring Simple Fielding already displays. **Quiz** is retrieval practice. It freezes at step N, has the player drag the token, scores by distance to a target zone, replays the correct move, and resurfaces misses on expanding intervals stored in IndexedDB. All of that runs on the device. A coach can assign a quiz with a share link that encodes play IDs and position, but cannot see results without a backend. Players would have to send back a screenshot or a results link. Set that expectation honestly rather than building a server under deadline. It is also the more privacy-friendly design.

## Softball depth, field levels, and polish separate a maintained board from an abandoned one

Content breadth is where the strongest competitors are ahead. Simple Fielding covers Little League (60 ft, no leadoffs), 50/70, 13U–14U, high school, college, and pro, plus softball from 8U coach pitch through college. It also has slapper defense, the look-back rule, and delayed steals ([Simple Fielding](https://simplefielding.com/)). Pitch MetRx switches between baseball and softball fields in one tap ([Pitch MetRx](https://www.pitchmetrx.com/coaches-whiteboard/)). InMotion's subscription business rests on 300+ softball rotations ([InMotion](https://inmotionplaybooks.com/softball-membership-page/)). A softball field variant with slap and bunt plays, and at least a 60-ft youth field, would close the largest content gap. These are larger than a ten-week item if the library needs re-authoring, so they belong at the front of the post-launch queue unless the field geometry is already parameterized.

The adjacent tactics boards set the polish bar. TacBoard offers curve control on movement paths and ball/player trails ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/themanagerboard-tacboard/6763226753)). Baseball Board – Tactics offers curved, wavy, and dashed lines, zones, text labels, and 50-step redo ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6763575410)). Hudl saves a template of every card so coaches "never draw a play or formation more than once" ([Hudl](https://www.hudl.com/products/playtools)). The most useful of these for Diamondboard are redo, text labels (a complaint against Baseball WhiteBoard), "start a new play from this library play," a local roster that puts names or numbers on tokens, and a projector/AirPlay-friendly presentation mode. Curved paths and trails are post-launch polish.

Trust in local-only data needs attention before launch, because Diamondboard has no cloud to fall back on. Since iOS 17, Home Screen web apps get the same large quota as Safari and can request persistent storage, which WebKit grants by heuristics that favor installed apps ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/?p=14403)). But storage is **not shared between Safari and the installed app** ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)), and deleting the Home Screen icon removes its data. Brave on iOS still appears to lack its own "Add to Home Screen," so users have to install through Safari ([Brave community](https://community.brave.app/t/add-to-home-screen-as-pwa-standalone-web-app-on-ios/650126)). Before launch, Diamondboard needs four things. It should call `navigator.storage.persist()` on first standalone launch. It should offer an "export all plays" JSON backup and restore. It should add a confirm step and a "recently deleted" bin for My Plays; TacticalPad ships a Recycle Bin, and a reviewer asked for it to be "more difficult to delete" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tacticalpad-coachs-whiteboard/id512949303)). And it should show an install banner that tells Brave and in-browser Safari users how to open the app in Safari and add it to the Home Screen, and warns them that plays saved in a tab will not carry over. Privacy is a selling point here too. The amended COPPA Rule has been fully in force since **April 22, 2026** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule), [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/coppa-rule-amendment-compliance-deadline-approaches)). A short "nothing leaves your device" page, no analytics SDKs, and a warning before player names go into shared links all help. This is not legal advice.

The research did not find category-specific sources on accessibility or Spanish. The general recommendations still apply post-launch: a non-drag alternative for moving tokens and answering quizzes, position letters as well as color, and externalized strings with a coach-reviewed Spanish pass.

## A prioritized roadmap for a small team

The table orders the work by impact on launch-day competitiveness relative to cost, and marks what needs a server.

| Priority | Feature | When | Client-only? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Share a play as a URL-fragment link (+ client-side QR) | Pre-launch | Yes | Table stakes at Simple Fielding and Baseball Board – Tactics; reaches parents and assistants |
| 2 | PNG export and Web Share of the current step or play | Pre-launch | Yes | Gets plays into GameChanger/TeamSnap chats; low risk |
| 3 | JSON backup/restore, `storage.persist()`, delete confirm and Recently Deleted | Pre-launch | Yes | No-cloud safety net; documented complaint |
| 4 | Install guidance for Brave and in-browser Safari; privacy page; first-run tutorial | Pre-launch | Yes | Brave can't install; separate storage; "no tutorial" complaint |
| 5 | Position Study v1 (highlight a position, show its notes, ball/base/backup coloring) | Pre-launch | Yes | Reuses steps and notes; matches Simple Fielding focus mode |
| 6 | Print/PDF playbook via print CSS | Pre-launch if time, else first post-launch | Yes | Proven with no server by Football Play Card and FastDraw |
| 7 | Quiz: freeze, drag, score, replay; spaced resurfacing in IndexedDB; assignment links | Post-launch (Q1 2027) | Yes, except coach-visible results | Whitespace: quizzes on the team's own playbook |
| 8 | Softball field and slap/look-back plays; 60-ft and 50/70 youth fields | Post-launch (before spring seasons) | Yes | Largest content gap against Simple Fielding and InMotion |
| 9 | Redo, text labels, "new play from library play," local roster labels, presentation mode | Post-launch | Yes | Polish and convenience parity |
| 10 | MP4 video export with a PNG-per-step fallback | Post-launch stretch | Yes, device-dependent | Valued but WebKit-risky; needs on-device tests |
| 11 | Wristband and play-card layouts; curved paths and trails | Post-launch | Yes | Distinctive borrowings from football and soccer boards |
| 12 | Accessibility alternatives and Spanish localization | Post-launch | Yes | General best practice; not sourced for this category |
| 13 | Coach dashboards for quiz results, read receipts, cloud sync, push reminders | Later, only if demand is proven | **No**: needs accounts and a backend; Web Push needs a push server ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)) | Hudl and CoachNow territory; conflicts with the no-account promise |
| 14 | Paid "Coach Pro" unlock or App Store wrapper | Later, optional | **Partly**: an offline Ed25519 license check works in Safari 17+ ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_subtlecrypto_verify_ed25519)) but issuing keys needs a small serverless webhook; wrappers risk Guideline 4.2 rejection ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/835422)) | Free fits the rec-coach market best |

On pricing, the evidence favors staying free at launch. The strongest direct competitor markets "no ads, no accounts, no subscriptions" ([Simple Fielding](https://simplefielding.com/)). GameChanger gives coaches premium features free and charges fans ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gamechanger/id1308415878)). Even a $4.99 one-time app drew a "little expensive" first reaction ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id633644009?l=en-us)). Single-coach tactics boards that charge do so at roughly $20–25/yr and gate export behind it ([TacticalPad](https://apps.apple.com/app/id512949303)), so free export is itself a competitive weapon. If revenue matters later, a one-time unlock or team pack fits a no-recurring-cost app better than a subscription. The research found no direct evidence on what high school and travel programs would pay.

## Conclusion

Diamondboard is not losing on its core whiteboard; it is losing on reach. Every serious competitor treats the iPad as the start of a play's life, not the end. Because the browser can now compress, share, rasterize, and print entirely on-device, Diamondboard can match that reach without breaking its no-backend, no-account promise. That promise doubles as its COPPA and privacy story. The longer-term advantage lies in a combination no competitor has assembled: quizzes generated from the coach's own animated plays, with per-step "what and why" notes as the answer key, scheduled with spaced retrieval. The generic IQ apps cannot do this because they don't hold the team's playbook, and the whiteboards cannot because they lack a learning loop.

The main uncertainty is timing. If Simple Fielding ships its native app before Diamondboard has share links and Position Study, Diamondboard becomes the second free, private, iPad-first board in a niche small enough that second place is costly. A narrow, reliable launch with sharing, backup, and Position Study is a stronger position than a broad launch with fragile video export. Three things need on-device testing before the share design is locked: URL and QR length limits, iOS acceptance of a JSON play file through the share sheet, and MediaRecorder behavior on iOS 26 and Brave.
